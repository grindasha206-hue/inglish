I have to …

A · I have to do something = мне необходимо, я обязан

I have to do something = it is necessary for me to do it, I am obliged to do it

to wear etc.
  • I'll be late for work tomorrow. I have to go to the doctor.
  • Kate starts work at 7 o'clock, so she has to get up at 6.
  • You have to pass a test before you can get a driving licence.

B · Прошлое (yesterday / last week etc.) — had to …

  • I was late for work yesterday. I had to go to the doctor.
  • We had to wait for a taxi last night. There were no buses.

  • What time do you have to go to the doctor tomorrow?

I don't have to (do something) = в этом нет необходимости:

  • I'm not working tomorrow, so I don't have to get up early.
  • Max doesn't have to work very hard. He's got an easy job.
  • We didn't have to wait long for the bus — it came in two minutes.

D · must и have to

must или have to можно использовать, когда вы считаете что-то необходимым, когда даёте своё мнение:

  • It's a fantastic book. You must read it. или You have to read it.

Когда это не ваше личное мнение (это факт, правило), используйте have to (не must). Compare:

  • Kate won't be at work this afternoon. She has to go to the doctor. (это не моё мнение — это факт)
  • Kate isn't well. She doesn't want to go to the doctor, but I told her she must go. (это моё мнение)

A nurse's life

Что Ирине приходится делать на работе? Найдите have to, has to, had to.

Being a nurse is not easy, says Irina. She works in a big hospital, and she has to start at seven in the morning, so she has to get up at half past five.

She has to wear a uniform, and she has to be very careful with medicines. Sometimes she has to work at night or at weekends.

Last week was very hard. A colleague was ill, so Irina had to work four nights in a row. She didn't have to cook — her husband did everything at home.

'Do you have to work this Sunday?' I asked. 'No, I don't, thank goodness. I don't have to do anything. I'm going to sleep all day!'
