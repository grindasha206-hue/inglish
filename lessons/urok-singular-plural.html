train(s) bus(es) (singular and plural)

A · Множественное число: -s

Множественное число обычно -s:

singular (= one)→plural (= two or more)
a flower→some flowers
a train→two trains
one week→a few weeks
a nice place→some nice places
this student→these students
a flower
some flowers

Spelling:

-s / -sh / -ch / -x→-esbus → buses   dish → dishes
church → churches   box → boxes
alsopotato → potatoes   tomato → tomatoes
-y→-iesbaby → babies   dictionary → dictionaries   party → parties
but -ay / -ey / -oy→-ysday → days   monkey → monkeys   boy → boys
-f / -fe→-vesshelf → shelves   knife → knives   wife → wives

B · Эти вещи — во множественном числе

scissors
glasses
trousers
jeans
shorts
tights
pyjamas
  • Do you wear glasses?
  • Where are the scissors? I need them.

Можно сказать a pair of scissors / a pair of trousers / a pair of pyjamas etc.:

  • I need a new pair of jeans. or I need some new jeans. (не a new jeans)

C · Множественное число без -s

this man → these menone foot → two feetthat sheep → those sheep
a woman → some womena tooth → all my teetha fish → a lot of fish
a child → many childrena mouse → some mice

also a person → two people / some people / a lot of people etc.:

  • She's a nice person.
  • but They are nice people. (не nice persons)

D · people и police — множественное число

People — множественное число (= they), поэтому people are / people have etc.:

  • A lot of people speak English. (не speaks)
  • I like the people here. They are very friendly.

Police — множественное число:

  • The police want to talk to anybody who saw the accident. (не The police wants)

A shopping day

Найдите все слова во множественном числе. Какие без -s?

Last week I went shopping with my two children. First we bought some new clothes: two pairs of jeans, some shorts and a pair of pyjamas for my son.

Then we went to the market. We bought some potatoes, tomatoes and a lot of fish. My daughter wanted some knives for the kitchen.

At the farm near the market there were a lot of sheep and two monkeys! The children loved them. There were a lot of people there.

In the evening my feet were tired. My son said: 'Where are my glasses?' 'Call the police!' he joked. But we didn't need help — his glasses were on his head!
